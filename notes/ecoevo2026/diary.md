# Class diary

What we covered in each class, with links to the corresponding sections of the notes.

**Tue 22/9** — Logistics and exam, then the shape of the whole course: [what we will write, and what it leaves out](l0.html#what-we-will-write-and-what-it-leaves-out). We built exponential growth from [the simplest scenario, birth and death](l1.html#the-simplest-scenario-birth-and-death), saw the same thing in [discrete generations](l1.html#discrete-generations), and put numbers on it — [orders of magnitude](l1.html#orders-of-magnitude) and [what fraction of all humans ever born is alive today](l1.html#what-fraction-of-all-humans-ever-born-is-alive-today). The rest of the class was about why identical cultures do not grow identically: [the observation](l2.html#the-observation), [what a rate is](l2.html#what-a-rate-is), and the fact that [the mean obeys the deterministic equation](l2.html#the-mean-obeys-the-deterministic-equation). We wrote the [master equation](l2.html#master-equation), and of the [probability generating function](l2.html#probability-generating-function) only the idea, not the derivation.

**Thu 24/9** — We went back to [the observation](l2.html#the-observation) of stochasticity in trajectories and asked what the mean misses: [the second moment and the variance](l2.html#the-second-moment-and-the-variance), the [expected number of deaths](l2.html#expected-number-of-deaths) and the [extinction probability](l2.html#extinction-probability). We derived those from birth death process and compared with experiment. We closed with how to simulate the process exactly, [the Gillespie algorithm](l2.html#simulating-exactly-with-gillespie). [What survivors look like](l2.html#what-survivors-look-like) is left to the notes.

**Tue 29/9** — Mutations. From the [biology](l3.html#biology-intro) (bacteria crossing bands of antibiotic, and the two hypotheses: resistance acquired on contact, or already there by chance) to the [setup](l3.html#setup) of Luria and Delbrück's experiment. Under [acquired resistance](l3.html#hypothesis-a.-acquired-resistance-lamarckian) the counts are Poisson. Under [spontaneous mutation](l3.html#hypothesis-b.-spontaneous-mutation) we computed the mean, wrote [the exact recursion](l3.html#the-exact-recursion), and approximated the distribution by following the first mutant: [heuristics and approximation of the tail](l3.html#heuristics-and-approximation-of-the-tail) and its [moments](l3.html#moments). Same mean, very different fluctuations. The end was rushed and it will be recapped next class.

**Mon 5/10** — First a slower recap of Luria and Delbrück: from the [first mutant](l3.html#heuristics-and-approximation-of-the-tail) to the $1/M^2$ tail and its [moments](l3.html#moments), checked against simulations, the 1943 data and a modern version of the experiment with DNA barcodes. Then we started [L4](l4.html), [limits to growth](l4.html): the exponential curves of L1 eventually flatten, [Malthus](l4.html#malthus)' argument, and Verhulst's [logistic growth](l4.html#logistic-growth-as-an-effective-model), solved exactly and made dimensionless. We stopped before the [graphical method](l4.html#graphical-method-for-long-term-behavior), where the next class starts.

**Wed 7/10** — Long-term behaviour without solving the equation: the [graphical method](l4.html#graphical-method-for-long-term-behavior) and [fixed points and their stability](l4.html#fixed-points-and-their-stability), by linearization. Then we made the limit to growth explicit: [growth on limited resources](l4.html#growth-on-limited-resources), with the yield and a growth rate that depends on the resource concentration, whose shape we derived from the kinetics of transporters and enzymes ([why growth saturates](l4.html#why-growth-saturates)) and compared with Monod's data. We wrote the equations of a population consuming its resource; their analysis is next class.
